flappyboard in the living room

2026-07-28 · features: tv-pairing (new), family-grants (new), kiosk-display (new) · draft · round 0

TL;DR

The board already runs fine on a TV — what does not work is getting it onto the TV and keeping it there: /b/:boardId demands the owner's login, so somebody types an email and password with a D-pad, and every phone re-scans the QR twice a day because controller grants expire in 12 hours. Fix that with a device-code pairing flow (TV shows a code, owner approves from their phone), long-lived revocable family grants, and a kiosk-hardening pass on the display route. Explicitly not a native Tizen/webOS app — that buys an icon and autostart for weeks of certificate wrangling, and a $40 stick in kiosk mode buys the same two things in an afternoon.

Context

Per plan 2026-07-27-flappyboard-mvp (reviewed, 2 rounds), the MVP shipped the split-flap board, the QR phone controller, and the LLM walkie-talkie. The 2026-07-28 checkpoint records v0.1.0 released — PR #2 squash-merged as f8ae90f and tagged. That same checkpoint names two outstanding owner-only tasks that this plan is the natural home for: "drive voice on a real phone over HTTPS, walk the display on the actual Samsung TV." Nobody has yet put this on the TV it was designed for.

Feature state: 8 shipped, feat-009 llm-board-agent still in-progress — deliberately held open until it has a browser-level verdict doc. The scope policy allows exactly one in-progress feature, so decision 6 below asks how to sequence this work against it.

What already exists and gets reused rather than rebuilt (from feat-008 phone-control, verdict PASS, 10 screenshots):

  • app/lib/board/pairing.ts — HMAC-SHA256 mint/verify over crypto.subtle, signed message prefix|boardId.length|boardId|payload for domain separation and MAC-audience binding, verify order structure→signature→claims, constant-time compare, 512-char ceiling before crypto, injected clock. Two token families already: PAIRING_PREFIX = "fbp1" and GRANT_PREFIX = "fbg1".
  • Single-use redemption enforced inside the board Durable Object under blockConcurrencyWhile — measured at 6 concurrent redemptions returning 200 401 401 401 401 401, and still refused after a full dev-server restart, proving it is storage-backed.
  • grantEpoch on the board row — bumping it kills every outstanding grant at once. This is already the "kick everyone off" primitive.
  • HttpOnly cookie per board, fb_grant_<boardId> (GRANT_COOKIE_PREFIX).
  • The display route already handles a TV remote: any keydown unlocks audio (display.tsx:160), and QR re-minting uses setInterval + visibilitychange and nothing else, with a comment reading "so this works on a Tizen browser."

So this plan is mostly a third token family and a mirror-image flow, not new cryptography.

Big picture

Today the QR flow assumes the TV is already authenticated and the phone is not. Device-code pairing is the same trust transfer running the other way: the phone holds the session and hands authority to the TV.

The two epochs are separate on purpose: revoking family controllers should not black out the TV, and un-pairing a TV should not log out the family.

Decisions needed

1. How does the TV authenticate to /b/:boardId?

Today display.tsx:52 calls requireSession. That means typing an email and password on a D-pad keyboard, and re-typing it whenever the TV browser evicts cookies. This is the single biggest source of friction in the product.

OptionProsCons
TV types one short URL, ever. Familiar (Netflix/YouTube shape, RFC 8628). Reuses the whole pairing.ts HMAC surface as a third prefix. Revocable per-device. New route pair + a code store in the DO. Short codes are low entropy — needs rate limiting and a short TTL (see error paths).
Cheapest to build — one more token prefix, no new UI at all. A ~60-char secret typed on a D-pad is worse than the problem. Bearer-in-URL leaks via history, screenshots, and the QR the board itself prints.
Zero work. The status quo, which is the thing being complained about. Also puts a full owner session on a shared living-room device.

2. What actually runs the display in the living room?

You asked whether to make this a TV app. This card is that question.

OptionProsCons
~$40. Autostarts on boot, HDMI-CEC can wake the TV, real Chromium (WebSocket keepalive, Wake Lock, fullscreen all behave). Stays a plain web app — zero platform-specific code. One more box behind the TV. A hardware dependency in a software plan.
No extra hardware. Works today. No kiosk mode, so browser chrome stays visible. No autostart — someone picks up the remote after every power cycle. Aggressive cookie eviction, which is exactly what decision 1 is trying to survive.
Home-screen icon and autostart, the "real app" feel. Samsung dev account, signing cert, TV in dev mode with your PC's IP whitelisted, sideload certs that expire, store review if you ever want it to survive. Buys exactly the two things the stick buys, for weeks instead of an afternoon. not recommended

3. How long does a family phone stay paired?

DEFAULT_GRANT_TTL_SECONDS = 12 * 60 * 60 (pairing.ts:61) — pair after breakfast, re-scan after dinner.

OptionProsCons
A phone used weekly never re-scans. A guest's phone ages out by itself. grantEpoch still revokes everything instantly. Renewal-on-use is a new code path (re-mint + Set-Cookie on the socket upgrade).
One-constant change. Genuinely trivial. Everyone in the house re-scans on the same day, forever.
Never re-scan. A lost phone holds board access forever unless someone remembers to bump the epoch. No natural expiry is a bad default even at home.

4. Do controllers have names?

With several family phones paired, "who put that on the board" becomes a real question — both for delight and for revocation.

OptionProsCons
Makes per-device revoke meaningful ("un-pair Kai's phone" beats "bump the epoch and re-pair everyone"). One text field, once. Needs a per-grant record in the DO instead of a pure stateless token.
Fun. Family chat energy. Spends a row of a 6-row board on metadata, and the LLM feature already competes for those rows.
Nothing to build. Keeps grants stateless. Revocation stays all-or-nothing.

5. Burn-in and always-on behaviour.

A split-flap board is static high-contrast text held for hours — close to a worst case for an OLED panel, and unflattering on any panel at 3am.

OptionProsCons
Drift (a few px on a slow cycle) is invisible to a viewer and is the standard mitigation. Dimming overnight is what a real board in a hallway would do anyway. Two behaviours to tune. Drift interacts with the vmin layout and must not trigger a scrollbar.
Simpler; one schedule. Does nothing for the 16 waking hours the board holds one message.
No work. Fine on LCD/QLED. On OLED this is a slow, permanent, expensive mistake.

6. Sequencing against feat-009 llm-board-agent.

The scope policy permits one in-progress feature. feat-009 is open, awaiting a browser-level verdict doc, and issue #1 (rate limiting / spend caps on board.generate and /api/transcribe) still blocks any deploy with a real key. Rate limiting is also a prerequisite for this plan — a device-code endpoint needs it too.

OptionProsCons
Obeys the scope policy without a special case. Issue #1's rate limiter gets built once and this plan inherits it. The TV walk that feat-009 still owes is the same walk this plan needs anyway. Delays the living-room work by that closeout.
Living-room work starts immediately. Policy-legal — "mark the first blocked with a reason in evidence." Leaves a nearly-done feature parked, and rate limiting still has to happen before either ships for real.
Unblocks both, is small, and is the only thing genuinely gating a real-key deploy. A third work item between you and a board on the wall.

Plan of record

Double-click any phase to rewrite it; x to drop it.

  1. Phase 0 — prerequisite: rate limiting. Land issue #1 (spend caps on board.generate and /api/transcribe) and extend the same limiter to the new device-code endpoints. Acceptance: a fixed-window counter in the board DO; unit tests prove the Nth call in a window is refused; the limiter is reachable from both the tRPC procedure and the device-code redeem path.
  2. Phase 1 — TV device-code pairing. Add a third token family fbd1 to pairing.ts (mintDeviceGrant / verifyDeviceGrant, fb_device_<boardId> HttpOnly cookie). New /tv route displays a 6-char code; new /link route lets a signed-in owner approve it against a board; the code lives in the BoardRoom DO with a short TTL and single-use redemption under blockConcurrencyWhile, exactly like the pairing token. display.tsx accepts session OR device grant. Acceptance: a browser context holding zero cookies reaches a live board through /tv + /link alone; concurrent redemption of one code returns exactly one 200; typecheck 0; unit tests for the new token family mirror the existing pairing.test.ts cases.
  3. Phase 2 — family grants. Raise the controller grant TTL per decision 3, add sliding renewal on socket upgrade, and add an optional device name captured once at pairing. Owner gets a paired-devices list on /boards with per-device revoke alongside the existing bump-the-epoch revoke. Acceptance: a grant older than the old 12h ceiling still opens a socket; per-device revoke kills exactly one device and leaves the others live; grantEpoch bump still kills all of them.
  4. Phase 3 — kiosk hardening. navigator.wakeLock with a silent-looping-video fallback; requestFullscreen on the first gesture (the keydown handler at display.tsx:160 already exists — extend it); a watchdog that hard-reloads after the socket has been dead past a threshold; pixel drift and idle dim per decision 5. Acceptance: the page holds fullscreen and stays awake across an 8h unattended run; killing the socket triggers exactly one reload, not a loop; drift never produces a scrollbar (the existing verification already asserts scrollable=false).
    Why is fullscreen not just a CSS concern?

    The Fullscreen API requires a user gesture, and on a wall-mounted TV there may never be one after the initial setup. That is the same constraint the sound-unlock prompt already documents — which is why this rides the existing gesture handler rather than adding a second one.
  5. Phase 4 — the actual living room. Write the kiosk-device recipe (.brain/recipes/) for whichever runtime decision 2 picks, then do the owner-only walk this has been owing since 2026-07-28: the display on the real Samsung TV and voice on a real phone over HTTPS. Acceptance: a verification doc per brain playbook verify, with screenshots, covering pair-the-TV, send-from-phone, and revoke.

Golden path

Setup, once, on a TV that has never seen the app:

sequenceDiagram
  participant TV as TV / kiosk
  participant W as Worker
  participant DO as BoardRoom DO
  participant P as Owner phone
  TV->>W: GET /tv
  W->>DO: issueDeviceCode()
  DO-->>W: code "K7Q2XM" (TTL 5 min, single-use)
  W-->>TV: renders the code, starts polling
  P->>W: POST /link { code, boardId } (has session)
  W->>DO: approveDeviceCode(code, boardId, owner)
  DO-->>W: ok — code consumed under blockConcurrencyWhile
  TV->>W: poll
  W-->>TV: 200 + Set-Cookie fb_device_<boardId> (HttpOnly)
  TV->>W: GET /b/:boardId (device grant, no session)
  W-->>TV: the board

Everyday use, unchanged from today except that nobody re-scans: the TV prints the QR, a family phone lands on /b/:boardId/c, and its 30-day grant renews itself every time it connects.

Testing plan

Open questions

  • Should /tv poll, or should the TV hold a WebSocket to the DO and be told the moment it is approved? Polling is simpler and the TV already has a revalidation timer; a socket is nicer but the TV has no board to join yet.
  • Where does an unapproved device code live before it is bound to a board? It cannot be in a board's DO if no board is chosen yet — either a global codes DO, or /tv asks the owner to pick the board first and the code is board-scoped from birth.
  • Does the family need more than one board in the house (kitchen and living room), and if so does one phone grant cover both?
  • Is there an offline story? If the house internet drops, the board holds its last grid but the socket is gone — should it show anything, or stay silent and just reconnect?
